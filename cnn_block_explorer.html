What blocks 2 and 3 do with this

  • Block 1's output, [16, 14, 14], becomes block 2's input. That is why conv2 starts with 16.
  • Each of block 2's 32 filters looks at the same 3 × 3 spot on all 16 maps at once. A filter that wants "vertical line here and horizontal line here" finds corners.
  • Block 3 does the same with block 2's 32 maps, so its 64 filters can respond to whole shapes, like a closed loop at the top of a 9.
  • After block 3's pool, 64 grids of 3 × 3 are flattened into 576 numbers, and linear turns them into 10 digit scores.
